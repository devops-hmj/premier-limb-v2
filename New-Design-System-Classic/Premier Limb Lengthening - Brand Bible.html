§ 01 · Vol. I

Mission. Vision. Values. The non-negotiables that govern every decision downstream.

Mission & vision

Mission

What we do, every day.

Premier Limb Lengthening exists to deliver transformative height enhancement through surgical precision, concierge-level care, and an unwavering commitment to patient safety. We combine the clinical rigor of a Level I trauma center with the attentiveness of a private practice to help patients achieve measurable, life-changing results.

Vision

Where this is going.

To become the most trusted name in limb lengthening worldwide — the practice that other surgeons refer to, that patients travel continents to reach, and that sets the clinical standard for safety, outcomes, and patient experience in cosmetic orthopedic surgery.

Core values

Five values, in order of operational priority. They govern hiring, communication, surgical decision-making, and what we will and will not say in marketing.

01

Precision

Every millimeter matters. We measure twice, cut once, and obsess over outcomes that lesser practices accept as “good enough.”

02

Transparency

No hidden fees, no vague timelines, no sugarcoating risk. Patients deserve the full picture before they commit.

03

Craft

Surgery is not a commodity. Dr. Basmajian treats every procedure as a singular act of skill, refined through hundreds of cases.

04

Stewardship

We are temporary custodians of our patients’ trust, mobility, and wellbeing. That responsibility extends long past discharge.

05

Transformation

Height is the outcome. Confidence, presence, and a fundamentally different relationship with the world — that is the result.

— Brand Promise The most qualified hands. The most attentive care. The height you’ve always envisioned — delivered safely, precisely, and with the discretion you expect at this level.

05

Voice & tone.

§ 05 · Vol. I

We write like a periodical, not a brochure. The reader is treated as an intelligent adult, not a customer to be persuaded.

The three voice rules

№ 01i.

Editorial, not promotional

Decks, datelines, captions. No urgency theater. No exclamation marks. No ALL CAPS.

№ 02ii.

Evidenced, not asserted

Every claim earns its place with a number, credential, or named patient. Specificity is the proof.

№ 03iii.

Direct, not theatrical

Short sentences. Active voice. No hedging, no superlatives. The Premier Blue speaks volume; language stays measured.

Voice attributes

Clear, Not Clinical
Precise medical terminology when it adds clarity, then plain-language explanation.
Confident, Not Boastful
Let credentials speak. “Hundreds of procedures” beats “best surgeon in the world.”
Direct, Not Blunt
State risks without dramatizing. Benefits without overselling. $75K+ decisions deserve directness.
Warm, Not Casual
Approachable but professional. First-person plural. Never slangy.
Discreet, Not Evasive
Acknowledge privacy without making the decision feel shameful.

Worked examples

Procedure Page

Limb lengthening works by surgically placing a magnetic internal nail — the PRECICE system — inside the bone. Over 3–4 months, the nail gradually lengthens at approximately 1mm per day, controlled by an external remote. No external hardware is visible. The bone regenerates naturally in the gap — a process called distraction osteogenesis. Results are permanent.

Brand story.

§ 06 · Vol. I

Origin, proof, and the path from trauma bay to private practice.

The origin

Dr. Hrayr Basmajian did not start in cosmetic surgery. He started in the emergency room in Detroit, drawn to the speed, stakes, and immediate impact of trauma medicine. When he rotated through orthopedics in medical school and encountered his first pelvic fracture case, the direction of his career became clear.

He trained at USC/LAC Medical Center — one of the highest-volume trauma residencies in the country — then completed a trauma fellowship at Sonoran Orthopaedic Trauma Surgeons in Scottsdale, followed by additional training in Hannover, Germany.

He became Chair of Orthopaedic Trauma at Loma Linda University Medical Center before being recruited to Pomona Valley Hospital Medical Center as Orthopedic Trauma Medical Director.

Through years of trauma work — rebuilding shattered limbs, correcting deformities — Dr. Basmajian developed particular expertise in limb lengthening. He noticed the transformation was as much psychological as physical. He also noticed patients arriving with complications from procedures performed elsewhere. The need for a practice built on clinical excellence became undeniable.

Premier Limb Lengthening is the result: a surgeon with the credentials of an academic trauma director delivering the attention of a concierge physician.

Photography & art direction.

§ 08 · Vol. II

Editorial portraiture from a medical journal — not stock, not lifestyle, not clinical sterility.

Photography philosophy

Every image answers the implicit question: “Is this someone I can trust with a $75K–$150K decision about my body?” The visual register is warm authority — real environments, real light, real surgeon.

Photography categories

Plate 01 · Portrait
Category 01

Surgeon portraits

The primary brand asset. Warm lighting, ¾ turn or direct gaze. White coat or scrubs. Never arms crossed.

Plate 02 · Suite
Category 02

Clinical environment

Skytron Glass OR, consultation rooms, imaging equipment. Modern, institutional depth. Not cold — precise.

Plate 03 · Moment
Category 03

Patient moments

Recovery milestones, consultations (with consent). Candid, editorial, respectful. Hands, posture — not faces unless consented.

Plate 04 · Place
Category 04

Facility & location

Exterior architecture. Southern California environment. Aspirational SoCal — coastline, modern recovery suite. Supports concierge.

Lighting & color grading

Warm but controlled. Natural light preferred, supplemented with soft directional lighting. Slightly desaturated, cool shadows, warm highlights. Never over-processed, never HDR, never lifestyle-bright. The mood is quiet confidence, not aspirational energy.

Composition rules

Editorial direction
  • 01Generous negative space. Let the subject breathe.
  • 02Shallow depth of field for surgeon portraits.
  • 03Rule of thirds, not centered. Editorial, not corporate.
  • 04Monospace text overlays (Plate 01, Plate 02) in editorial contexts.
  • 05Horizontal for web hero. Square for social. Vertical for mobile.

Photography don'ts

Breaks the register
  • 01Stock medical photography. No generic doctors, no handshakes, no clipboards.
  • 02Patients in hospital gowns without clinical context + consent.
  • 03Before/after imagery without written patient consent.
  • 04Bright, warm, lifestyle-wellness color grading. This is not a spa.
  • 05Over-composed group shots. PLL is one surgeon, not a team photo op.

The intelligence layer.

§ 12 · Vol. II

Not a feature — a structural brand differentiator. The competitive window is open. No competitor has it.

Strategic context

The Intelligence Layer is a first-mover opportunity with a defined window. It reinforces every pillar: Authority (clinical precision in voice), Technology (the platform itself is cutting-edge), Concierge (the patient is never waiting for a response).

The three phases

Phase 01

Automated journey

2 – 3 weeks · GoHighLevel
  • Inquiry response. Auto-acknowledge in minutes. Personal follow-up <2 business hours.
  • Nurture sequence. 5-email educational drip over 10–14 days.
  • Post-consult. 3-email follow-up with plan recap, financing, open door.
  • Post-op milestones. Week-by-week triggers from surgery date.
  • Reactivation. 30/60/90-day check-ins, educational not salesy.
  • Pipeline. Inquiry → Qualified → Booked → Pre-Op → Post-Op → Alumni.
Phase 02

AI clinical concierge

Months 3 – 6 · Text-based
  • Design philosophy. Clinical instrument, not chatbot. Ink background. Monospace metadata. Serif AI responses.
  • Procedure Q&A. Curated knowledge base — never open-ended generation.
  • Candidacy pre-screen. Age, BMI, conditions, expectations. Indicates appropriateness — does not diagnose.
  • Pricing transparency. Same numbers as the site. No bait-and-switch.
  • Handoff. When scope exceeded, says so clearly and offers direct connection.
Phase 03

AI video avatar

Months 6 – 12 · HeyGen / Synthesia
  • Surgeon digital clone. Patient education at scale, multilingual.
  • Uses. Procedure explanation, FAQ responses, pre-consultation prep, international comms.
  • Production parity. Same lower thirds, title cards, color grading.
  • Disclosure. Every AI-generated video carries a non-hidden label.
— Voice Calibration The AI speaks in the same voice rules: editorial, evidenced, direct. No emoji. No exclamation marks. No urgency. It addresses the patient as an intelligent adult. It acknowledges uncertainty. It never makes claims the surgeon has not made.

18

Accessibility & compliance.

§ 18 · Vol. III

Not optional. An expression of the stewardship core value.

Accessibility standards

All PLL digital properties must meet WCAG 2.1 AA compliance. This is a brand standard.

Color Contrast
All text 4.5:1 contrast (AA). Premier Blue on white passes. Gold on white does not. Editorial Gold should never be used for text on light backgrounds.
Alt Text
Every image requires descriptive alt text. Decorative images use empty alt attributes.
Keyboard Navigation
All interactive elements must be keyboard-accessible. Focus states must be visible.
Screen Reader
Semantic HTML. Proper heading hierarchy. ARIA labels where semantic HTML is insufficient.
Motion
Respect prefers-reduced-motion. No auto-playing video. Animations must be pausable.

International compliance

GDPR (EU)
Geo-aware consent management (CookieYes recommended). Cookie banner before tracking. Right to erasure honored.
CCPA (California)
Privacy policy includes CCPA disclosures. “Do Not Sell” link in footer.
Medical Disclaimers
Risk disclosure on every procedure page. “Results may vary.” No guarantees of outcomes.
Privacy Policy
International language covering EU, UK, and California residents at minimum.
Medical Schema
Schema.org/MedicalProcedure and /Physician markup. Supports AEO and rich search results.

Standardized usage. Anyone creating content for PLL should reference this list before publishing.

The Procedure
“limb lengthening” or “height enhancement.” Never “leg lengthening surgery” or “bone breaking.”
The Technology
PRECICE system (capitalize), PRECICE nail, PRECICE MAX (4th gen). Always note “internal” and “no external hardware.” Manufacturer: NuVasive (do not emphasize).
The Surgeon
Dr. Basmajian (first reference: Dr. Hrayr Basmajian, MD, MS). Never “Doc B” or informal variants.
The Practice
Premier Limb Lengthening or PLL (internal only). Never abbreviate in patient-facing materials.
The Parent Practice
Premier Orthopaedic & Trauma Specialists. Referenced as institutional depth, not as the primary brand.
Patients
Always “patients.” Never “clients,” “customers,” or “cases.”
Recovery
“Recovery protocol” or “recovery timeline.” Never “downtime.”
Cost
“Investment” when discussing value. “Pricing” when being transparent about numbers. Never “cost” in isolation.
Height Gain
Stated as a range with context: “2–3 inches per bone segment” or “up to 5–6 inches with staged procedures.” Never a single number without context.
Consultation
Always “confidential consultation.” “Confidential” is a deliberate brand choice for the privacy-conscious buyer.
Concierge Program
The white-glove travel and recovery coordination service. Always described as a program, not a perk.
Revision Cases
“Revision surgery” or “correction of prior procedures.” Framed as expertise, not as criticism of other surgeons.
AI Concierge
The text-based AI clinical interface. “clinical concierge” or “consultation assistant.” Never “chatbot.”
